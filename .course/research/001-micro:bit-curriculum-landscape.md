# micro:bit Curriculum Landscape Research

## Existing Curricula

### Official micro:bit / MakeCode Resources
- **MakeCode Intro to CS** — 14-week course for grades 6–8, written for teachers without CS background. Covers fundamentals through MakeCode blocks. Very comprehensive but long-form. ([makecode.microbit.org/courses/csintro](https://makecode.microbit.org/courses/csintro))
- **First Lessons with MakeCode** — Sequence of 6 introductory projects for new learners. Shorter, more accessible. ([microbit.org/teach/lessons](https://microbit.org/teach/lessons/))
- **micro:bit Classroom Resources** — Units of work for ages 7–14, organized by topic. ([microbit.org/teach/classroom-resources](https://microbit.org/teach/classroom-resources/))

### Robotics (Cutebot)
- **University of Liverpool Cutebot Resources** — 60–90 minute robotics lessons for Years 7–9 using Cutebot + micro:bit. Covers MakeCode editor, motor control, sensor input, line following. Designed for pairs of students sharing one Cutebot. ([outreach.csc.liv.ac.uk](https://outreach.csc.liv.ac.uk/resources/cutebots/))
- No other comprehensive Cutebot curricula found — most resources are product pages or single-lesson plans.

### Game Development (MakeCode Arcade)
- **MakeCode Arcade Intro to CS** — Full CS course built around game creation. Covers sprites, movement, collision, scoring, levels. ([arcade.makecode.com/courses/introcs](https://arcade.makecode.com/courses/introcs))
- **Code a Carnival** — Free resource: students create carnival games (Whack-the-Mole, etc.). Guided tutorials + teacher guide.
- **Cyber Arcade (Maker Ed)** — Middle school curriculum combining micro:bit hardware with MakeCode Arcade game development. Ages 12–14, little to no prior experience needed. ([makered.org](https://makered.org/resources/cyber-arcade-programming-and-making-with-microbit/))

### Code.org
- **Physical Computing with Code.org** — Maker curriculum integrating micro:bit with Code.org's CS curriculum. ([code.org/curriculum/maker](https://code.org/en-US/curriculum/maker))

## Gaps & Opportunities

1. **No single curriculum covers all three domains** (basic micro:bit + arcade games + robotics) in a modular, choose-your-path format. Most are either pure micro:bit OR pure Arcade OR pure robotics.
2. **Cutebot curriculum is thin** — Liverpool's resources are the main structured offering. There's a real gap for classroom-ready Cutebot content.
3. **Arcade + micro:bit integration is rare** — Cyber Arcade is the closest, but it's more of a program framework than a lesson-by-lesson curriculum.
4. **Modular/flexible structure is uncommon** — Most curricula are linear (do lesson 1, then 2, then 3). The "choose your track" model is a differentiator.

## What We Can Learn From

- **MakeCode Intro to CS** has great scaffolding — concepts build naturally. Worth referencing for the foundational modules.
- **Liverpool Cutebot resources** validate the 60–90 min session format for robotics and the pair-programming approach.
- **Cyber Arcade** confirms the appeal of combining physical making with game development for this age group.
