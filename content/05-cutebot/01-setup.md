Here is your Cutebot:

![Cutebot](https://www.elecfreaks.com/learn-en/_images/cutebot_01_01.jpg)

This tiny robot has a lot of interesting features that [you can learn about online](https://www.elecfreaks.com/learn-en/microbitKit/smart_cutebot/cutebot_car.html).

Your Cutebot can drive around, flash its lights, and avoid obstacles. Let's learn how to program it.

## Setup your Cutebot

To use the features of the Cutebot — to make it drive around — you will need to install an extension into your Micro:Bit editor. First, start a new project. If you are in an existing project, click on the ![Micro:Bit logo](/images/microbit.png) to go back to the main page, then click on the "+ New Project" button to start a new project. Let's call the program "Dizzy Bot". Then in your new project, click on the "Extensions" button.

In the extensions window, use the search bar at the top to search for "Cutebot", then select the Cutebot extension. When you return to the project page, your blocks menu in the middle of the page should have a new Cutebot entry.

Now insert your Micro:Bit into the Cutebot. Make sure that the grid of LEDs of the Micro:Bit is pointing toward the front.

![Cutebot with Micro:Bit](https://www.elecfreaks.com/learn-en/_images/cutebot_01_01.jpg)

Look on the back of the Cutebot for the switch:

![Cutebot top view](https://images.jointheleague.org/microbit/cutebot-top.png)

When you are programming the Cutebot, or anytime you don't want it to move, you should turn the switch off. The Cutebot will get some power when it is plugged into USB, but not as much as when the switch is on.
